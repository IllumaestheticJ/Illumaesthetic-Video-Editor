Built from a survey of the three Dropbox roots (`1. Epsilon Plus Videos`, `2. Illumaesthetic Videos`,
`3. Epsilon Motorsports`): every dump folder listed to the end, about 500 clips watched as contact sheets
out of roughly 2,500. Nothing in Dropbox was changed.

## Read this first

- **No speech has been heard.** There is no transcription tool in this environment, so every interview and
  piece-to-camera was identified by framing and audio level only. Scripts below are therefore written as
  voice-over that works on its own, with marked slots (`SYNC`) where the recorded speech should replace it
  once it is transcribed. Anything marked `[CONFIRM]` is a fact I could not read from the footage or the scan
  files and will not put on screen or in narration until you supply it.
- **Voice.** The posted long-forms are presenter-led (host to camera, his voice over b-roll). New narration
  needs either the host recording the VO script, or a preset AI voice. I won't clone a real person's voice.
- **Sampling.** The longest takes (5–17 GB locked-off shots, 81 GB of Grimace GoPro) were not watched end to
  end. Each pitch lists exactly which clips were seen.
- **House rules applied:** no text over IRL footage, only numbers read from the data, IRL→scan is a hard 1:1
  cut on a beat, parts only open or come off if a scan of that state exists, Epsilon videos end on the
  Epsilon+ wordmark, music not reused from earlier reels, imperial units in narration.
- **Already published, so not pitched:** Integra DE4/DE5, E210 and GR Corolla, FE Civic and MR2 "3rd
  Adjustments" install tutorials; Honda Beat "Project: BB NSX" pt 1; the MR2 body-kit long-form (11 of its

  Prelude, RWB, EK, R33, CRX, FL5, STI, Integra, Lola, IS350, Civic and the 40-odd cars in `projects/`.

## Script (VO draft; `SYNC` = replace with the host's recorded line)

> **[1]** A full car. One scanner. One stopwatch. *(cut to scan)* This is what came out the other end.
> **[3]** `SYNC — host intro.` Bridge if needed: This is a Lexus IS350 F Sport. We scan cars every week, but
> we've never put a clock on it. So today the timer starts before the first drop of powder and doesn't stop
> until the last pass is done.
> **[4]** Step one is the least glamorous. Glossy paint and glass confuse a scanner, so the whole car gets a
> thin matte coat. It wipes off afterwards. Every panel, every lamp, every piece of glass.
> **[5]** Then the scanner. It only sees a patch at a time, so you walk the car in overlapping passes and
> watch the screen fill in. The big panels are quick. The corners, the grille and the lamps are where the
> time goes.
> **[6]** `[CONFIRM: time on the watch at this check]` in. The outside is nearly there.
> **[7]** What separates a scan you can design parts from and a scan that only looks good is everything you
> can't see standing up: sills, arches, the underside, the wheels. That means jack stands and lying on the
> floor.
> **[8]** Last pass. Stop the clock. `[CONFIRM: final time]`.
> **[9]** And here's the result. Twenty-four million, nine hundred ninety-nine thousand, nine hundred
> ninety-two triangles. We also pulled the rear reflector and scanned what's behind it, because that's the
> kind of surface a part has to bolt to.
> **[10]** This scan is on our site now. If you're designing for this car, start from the real thing.

**What I fill:** the nine scan shots (house silver on black), both 1:1 cuts, the title and the triangle-count
label, S-Log3 grade, music, and trimming the 7-minute intro once it is transcribed.
**Gaps and risks:** (a) the final time is unknown to me until the intro and the stopwatch frames are read —

renders, unless you have a screen recording of the alignment; (c) no bumper or panel was removed on camera,

## Script (VO draft)

> **[1]** The MR2 Spyder never had a hardtop that fits like it belongs there. So we made one.
> **[3]** `SYNC — interview on why.` Bridge: The Spyder is one of the lightest mid-engine cars you can buy,
> and it comes with a folding soft top. For a car that lives at the track, that's the first thing you want
> to change.
> **[4]** Before designing anything, the old roof has to come out, and everything it bolts to has to be
> understood. The frame, the latches at the header, the rear deck it folds into.
> **[5]** Then we scan the car with the roof gone. This is the part most people skip. A roof has to seal
> against the windshield header, both door glasses and the rear deck at the same time, and none of those
> surfaces are flat. With the scan, the top is designed against the real car.
> **[6]** Here's the opening, and here's the surface we built over it.
> **[7]** The prototype is 3D-printed in sections. It's too big for one print, so it's split, printed, and
> bonded back together.
> **[8]** First fit. `SYNC — reactions.` This is the moment you find out what the scan couldn't tell you:
> how it sits when two people actually push it into place.
> **[9]** A top that isn't latched is a sail. The hardware goes into the printed part here.
> **[10]** And then the slow part. Sand, fit, check the glass gap, take it off, repeat.
> **[11]** Next morning, in daylight. `SYNC — interview close.` `[CONFIRM: where the top is now — production
> material, price, release]`.
